# START HERE: how to use these document guides

These guides help you **write your own portfolio documents quickly**. Each guide explains what the document is for, what to put in it,
which facts about CPUTrade to use, and gives blank tables to fill in. They do **not** contain finished text: the writing, the ratings,
the dates and the opinions must be yours, in your own words. You will do it faster than you fear, because most of the thinking is done for you.

## The guides (one per required document)

| 04-Resource-Plan | Resource Plan: RACI chart and resource allocation | 2-3 pages |

## Who writes what (3 members)

| Member | Documents | Estimated time |
|---|---|---|
| Member 1 (project manager) | 02 Final Report, then assemble everything (09) | 45-60 min |
| Member 2 | 03 Stakeholder Matrix, 04 Resource Plan, 05 Risk Plan | 45-60 min |
| Member 3 | 06 Quality Plan, 07 Transition and Closure, 08 Reflection (team part), contribution page | 45-60 min |

Everyone writes their **own individual reflection** (in guide 08) and reviews one other person's document at the end.

## The fastest way to work (do these in this order)

1. **Read 01-System-Explained once (10 minutes, everyone).** You cannot write well about a system you cannot explain in your own words.
2. **Open your Term 1 and Term 2 submissions** (charter, stakeholder analysis, risk register, communication plan, backlog and user stories, wireframes, sprint plans, retrospectives, progress reports, change records). The final documents are *updates* of these: copy the parts that are still true, fix the parts that changed, add what is new.
3. **For each document:** read its guide's "Purpose" and "What to write" parts, fill the tables with your real information, then write the short paragraphs in your own words. Tick the checklist at the end of the guide.
4. **Cross-check (15 minutes).** Swap documents: do names, roles, dates and feature names match everywhere? Do the numbers match the contribution page and the RACI?
5. **Assemble (09).** One portfolio file, contribution page, signed declaration last.

## Writing rules that save marks

- **Your own words.** The brief says plagiarism, including AI-generated content, can mean zero marks. Use the guides as a map, not as text to paste. Keep your notes and drafts; you may be asked to explain anything you submit.
- **Be honest.** A limitation explained well scores better than a feature claimed that does not exist. The system has a one-listing simulated checkout but no multi-item cart or real payment processing; it also has no AI fraud detection or microservices. Say so, with reasons (see 02 and 01).
- **Be specific.** "We tested the system" earns little; give the current backend, checkout integration, frontend and browser test counts, plus any manual scenarios and defects you can evidence.
- **Keep it consistent.** The same team member names and roles, the same feature names, the same dates everywhere.
- **Use evidence.** Screenshots (in `demo/screenshots/`), the automated test suites, git history, sprint notes, the demo video.
- **Short paragraphs, tables and headings.** Markers skim.

## Getting tables into Word quickly

Open the guide in VS Code, press **Ctrl+Shift+V** (preview), select a table, copy, and paste into Word: the table keeps its shape. Or open the matching PDF and copy from there. Then replace the example rows with your own.

## If you run out of time

Priority order: Final Report, Risk Plan, Reflection, Stakeholder Matrix, Resource Plan (RACI), Quality Plan, Transition and Closure. Never skip the contribution page or the signed declaration. A shorter honest document is better than a missing one.
